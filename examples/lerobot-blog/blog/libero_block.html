What this curation is worth, measured closed-loop

Search and mining are only useful if the training set they produce makes a better robot. DROID has no simulator, so we measured that on LIBERO, a simulation benchmark where a policy can be scored by whether it completes the task. LIBERO is also very clean: 1,693 demonstrations of 40 tasks, every one a success with the right instruction attached. Real collections are not like that, so we broke it in three ways that turn up in real teleop data, each on a random 10% of the episodes:

  • Wrong instruction. The task label is swapped for another task from the same suite.
  • Jittery actions. Noise on the arm commands, a spike on 4% of frames, the gripper flipping on 2%.
  • Out of sync. The action stream runs 0.6 seconds ahead of the observations, like a timestamp bug.

The pixels never change. All three defects live in the tabular part of the data, which is where real logging bugs live too. Then we trained SmolVLA the same way four times, 40,000 steps each, and scored each model in the simulator on 10 rollouts of every task, 400 per model.

The mess cost 23 points. Three queries on the table got 14 back, and on the goal suite, where several tasks share one scene and differ only in the instruction, almost all of it: 48 to 85. The oracle shows what perfect cleaning would have bought: 80.2, so roughly 3 of the remaining 9 points come from the 33 bad episodes that slipped through and roughly 6 from training on 30% fewer episodes.

The three queries are the same mechanism as the columns above: one column and one rule per defect, thresholds set as robust z-scores within each task, and nothing that knows which episodes were broken.

  • Jitter gets a per-frame jerk_score, how much the arm command changes between frames. Episodes far above their task's median are flagged.
  • Out of sync gets a per-episode act_lag: how many frames you have to shift the commanded motion before it best explains the motion the robot actually made. Clean logs peak at zero.
  • Wrong instruction gets a per-episode goal_dist: how far the last frame's embedding sits from the median last frame of every other episode with the same label.

The weak spot is worth knowing. Almost every wrong label that slipped through is in the spatial suite, where all ten tasks end with the same bowl on the same plate, so the last frame cannot tell the labels apart and neither can a check built on it.

The mess here was injected. The three defect types are real ones, taken from what turns up in teleop collections, and the queries make no use of knowing they were there. The rates, 10% each, are ours. On your data the mix will differ, and so will which check does the work. The scripts that broke LIBERO and the ones that found the damage are in the companion repo.
